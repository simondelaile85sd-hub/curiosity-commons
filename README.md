# Curiosity Commons

**Public working architecture — release candidate prepared 15 September 2026**

Curiosity Commons is a developing social-infrastructure project about whether people can belong first, encounter one another without a productivity test, discover capacities without turning them into obligations, and move—if they choose—from curiosity and learning toward helping, teaching, or earning.

This repository is a curated public working record. It is not a recruitment page, a service offer, a validated intervention, a legal structure, or evidence that the model works.

## The core commitment

Belonging is not conditional on productivity.

That commitment has operational consequences:

- capacities may be made discoverable, but disclosure and use remain voluntary;
- contributing nothing on a given day remains a legitimate form of participation;
- refusing an invitation, task, match, or exchange carries no penalty;
- achievement, skill, effort, scarcity, and responsibility may differ without changing equal human standing;
- access to a risky, scarce, regulated, or specialist activity may be differentiated without creating ranks of personhood;
- maintenance, facilitation, care, and other hidden labour must be visible enough to prevent silent exploitation;
- money and commerce may support a Commons, but must not redefine membership or worth; and
- any pilot must be able to fail safely and informatively.

## The pathway

The current architecture uses a staged pathway rather than a compulsory ladder:

> **belong → encounter → discover → learn → practise → optionally help → optionally teach → optionally earn**

People may pause, repeat, skip, or leave the pathway. Earning is one possible outcome, not the purpose of belonging and not proof of success.

## What is current

The current first-pilot design is **Pilot A v0.2**: a manual-first, local, low-pressure test with one host site, approximately 10–30 adults, and an 8–12 week operating window. It tests a narrow mechanism: belonging-first participation, optional capacity visibility, and human-facilitated, mutual-opt-in connection.

Pilot A deliberately excludes participant-facing AI, points or credits, commerce, federation, social-security mechanisms, and professional-service substitution. Earlier material that placed an AI station inside the first pilot is superseded for Pilot A.

No recruitment should begin from this repository. Host authority, insurance and liability, work health and safety, volunteer/employment classification, conduct and safeguarding, accessibility, privacy, and facilitator capacity remain external gates.

## Claim discipline

The repository separates:

- **normative project commitments**: what the project chooses to protect;
- **established prior art**: ideas, institutions, and evidence that predate this project;
- **project synthesis**: the particular way Curiosity Commons combines elements;
- **hypotheses**: propositions Pilot A or later work could test;
- **unvalidated proposals**: designs that have not been externally reviewed or tested;
- **superseded material**: retained privately for history but not treated as current;
- **private or unreleased material**: excluded from the public release; and
- **AI-assisted operationalisation**: structuring, stress-testing, instrument drafting, and release preparation that does not create evidence or validation.

No novelty claim is made for components already present in community centres, neighbourhood houses, commons governance, cooperatives, mutual aid, volunteering, time banking, situated learning, or related practice. The combined configuration is a project synthesis whose distinctiveness and usefulness remain open questions.

## Human Foundations

[Human Foundations for an Age of AI](https://github.com/simondelaile85sd-hub/human-foundations-age-of-ai) is a broader and legally separate project. Curiosity Commons may operationalise and stress-test a subset of its questions. Neither project validates the other. Evidence transfers only to the specific mechanism or proposition actually tested.

## How to read or respond

Start with the architecture and claim register. Then read the Pilot A protocol together with—not apart from—the safeguards and measurement instruments. Critique is especially useful where it identifies a better existing institution, an inaccessible participation path, hidden labour, facilitator dependence, status capture, unsafe data collection, an untested assumption, or a reason the pilot should not proceed.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) before opening an issue or pull request. Do not post personal information, private correspondence, participant material, culturally restricted knowledge, or unpublished project archives.
