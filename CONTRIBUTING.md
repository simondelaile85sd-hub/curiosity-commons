# Contributing and critique guide

Curiosity Commons is at a pre-pilot, externally gated stage. The most useful contribution is often a clear reason to narrow, redesign, compare, pause, or stop.

## Useful critique

Please identify:

- an existing institution or literature that already covers a claimed component;
- an overstatement, missing citation, or incorrect evidence transfer;
- a contradiction between belonging and an operational rule;
- pressure created by “optional” contribution, matching, outreach, or recognition;
- hidden maintenance, care, access, or facilitation labour;
- founder, clique, property, funder, contributor, facilitator, data, or platform capture;
- an inaccessible path or a nominal choice that is not usable;
- an employment, volunteer, WHS, privacy, safeguarding, cultural-rights, or insurance issue;
- a better measure, competing explanation, adverse outcome, or failure threshold;
- an element that should remain outside Pilot A; or
- a reason an existing neighbourhood house, library, community centre, makerspace, or other setting is preferable.

## Claim discipline

When proposing a change, label it as one of:

- normative commitment;
- established prior art;
- project synthesis;
- hypothesis;
- operational proposal;
- external gate;
- supersession/correction; or
- evidence from an actual test.

Connect evidence to the exact proposition it supports. Do not use “innovative,” “proven,” “validated,” “safe,” “inclusive,” “scalable,” or “community-led” without specifying the evidence, population, context, reviewer, and limits.

## Public-boundary rules

Do not post:

- personal information or allegations about an identifiable person;
- participant experiences, case notes, images, recordings, or raw data;
- private messages, correspondence, archives, or unreleased project files;
- confidential host, legal, insurance, safety, or incident material;
- Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander knowledge or cultural material without the appropriate community authority and process;
- another person's questionnaire, standard, illustration, or substantial text without permission; or
- security-sensitive or safeguarding details that create risk.

For a sensitive concern, do not open a public issue. This release does not yet advertise a private reporting channel because no pilot or participant process is operating. A host-specific confidential route is a hard gate before recruitment.

## Pull requests

A pull request should state:

1. the precise problem;
2. the affected claim or mechanism;
3. the proposed status of the change;
4. sources and their limits;
5. privacy and rights implications;
6. whether a current file is corrected or superseded; and
7. what evidence would falsify or reverse the change.

By submitting material for inclusion, you confirm that you have the right to contribute it and agree that an accepted contribution may be distributed under the repository licence. Flag any required attribution or licence compatibility before submission.

## Conduct

Challenge the mechanism and the evidence directly. Do not demand personal disclosure as proof, rank contributors by credentials or output, or treat disagreement and refusal as lack of commitment. Safety concerns and disconfirming evidence receive priority over promotional consistency.
