# Project architecture

**Status:** Current project architecture, assembled from the most recent dedicated Curiosity Commons materials through 15 September 2026. Normative commitments are distinguished from empirical claims. The architecture has not been validated by a live pilot.

## 1. The organising question

Can a social setting make belonging real before usefulness is proven, while still helping people discover interests, capacities, relationships, and possible forms of contribution without turning discovery into obligation?

Curiosity Commons treats this as an institutional-design question, not as a slogan. A setting can say that everyone belongs while its rosters, praise, data, access rules, facilitator habits, or economic dependencies reward only visible contributors. The architecture therefore includes both a positive pathway and safeguards against the pathway becoming a ranking system.

## 2. Canonical commitments

For the current project, the following are design commitments rather than findings:

1. **Standing before productivity.** A person does not have to contribute, disclose a skill, accept a match, or pursue an outcome in order to belong.
2. **Capacity without conscription.** A capacity may become visible without becoming a duty, debt, identity, diagnosis, or availability promise.
3. **Voluntary contribution.** Help, maintenance, teaching, learning, exchange, and paid work require meaningful choice.
4. **Refusal without penalty.** Saying no must not reduce welcome, future access, social standing, or eligibility for unconditional parts of the Commons.
5. **Plural flourishing.** Quiet presence, companionship, inquiry, learning, making, care, contribution, teaching, enterprise, and rest can all be legitimate.
6. **Equal human standing.** Unequal achievement, experience, responsibility, scarcity, risk, or contribution does not create unequal personhood.
7. **Visible and challengeable power.** Formal and informal authority must be legible, reviewable, and contestable.
8. **Accessibility as usable freedom.** A nominal option is not voluntary if transport, sensory conditions, communication format, cost, scheduling, social intensity, or physical access make it unusable.
9. **Safety without deservingness.** Boundaries may attach to conduct, risk, consent, competence, or scarce resources—not to productivity-based judgments of worth.
10. **Commerce in service of the Commons.** Paid activity may support the setting but must not determine belonging or silently convert voluntary activity into unpaid labour.
11. **Data restraint.** The project should not create a reputation system, productivity history, inferred vulnerability profile, or durable dossier in order to coordinate ordinary participation.
12. **Dissent and exit.** Critique, nonparticipation, refusal, and leaving are legitimate signals, not disloyalty.
13. **Safe, informative failure.** A pilot is allowed to show that the mechanism is unhelpful, inferior to an existing institution, too burdensome, or unsafe to continue.

## 3. The staged pathway

The architecture is:

> **belong → encounter → discover → learn → practise → optionally help → optionally teach → optionally earn**

This is a map of possible movement, not an advancement ladder.

| Stage | Meaning | Protection against coercion |
|---|---|---|
| Belong | Be present without having to perform or disclose | Unconditional participation path; no contribution score |
| Encounter | Notice people, questions, activities, and possibilities | Quiet and low-social-intensity options remain legitimate |
| Discover | Recognise an interest, capacity, need, or resource | Self-description; optional and revisable; no diagnosis or inference |
| Learn | Receive explanation, demonstration, or companionship | Mutual consent; no debt created by receiving help |
| Practise | Try something at an appropriate risk level | Competence and safety boundaries without status ranking |
| Help | Offer time, care, knowledge, or practical assistance | Optional; material and recurring work crosses a labour review boundary |
| Teach | Support another person's learning | Recognition does not create automatic authority or permanent rank |
| Earn | Enter a clearly defined paid exchange | Separate terms, consent, accountability, and legal classification |

People may enter at different points, move in either direction, repeat a stage, stop, or remain at belonging. A successful encounter need not become a contribution. A discovered capacity need never be used.

## 4. Capacity discovery

The early capacity-discovery layer proposed a simple, participant-controlled board with prompts such as:

- I can…
- I enjoy…
- I need…
- I want to learn…
- I have…
- I am interested in…
- I am looking for someone to think, make, or do something with…
- I am happy just being here today.

“I enjoy” is important because ability is not willingness. Pilot A narrows the board further: entries are optional, temporary, removable, and do not form a rating or reputation history. Contact or introduction occurs only by mutual opt-in.

Candidate connection patterns include capacity-to-need, teacher-to-learner, resource-to-project, curiosity-to-curiosity, and a learner joining someone more experienced. These patterns are hypotheses about useful coordination, not entitlements to another person's time.

## 5. Plural exchange modes

The broader architecture allows several modes to coexist:

| Mode | What it is | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Gift | Help without a direct return | Social debt, overgiving, or hidden expectation |
| Direct exchange | A mutually agreed swap | Bargaining power and false equivalence |
| Commons reciprocity or mutual credit | Contribution recorded for later or indirect return | A universal score, surveillance, accumulation, or exclusion |
| Unconditional provision | Food, presence, or a basic activity available without exchange | Under-resourced maintenance or quiet rationing |
| Paid work | Clear compensation for labour, responsibility, risk, or expertise | Mission drift and a productivity-based membership hierarchy |
| Common pool | Resources held or stewarded for shared use | Capture, depletion, and unclear authority |

Pilot A tests none of the accounting modes. It has no points, credits, internal currency, commerce, or participant labour requirement. Plural exchange remains a broader design proposition.

## 6. The economic membrane

The economic membrane is a project synthesis: a boundary between the unconditional Commons and activities that require explicit economic terms.

It asks when an informal offer should become a paid, regulated, or otherwise formally accountable arrangement. Triggers include:

- substantial or repeated time;
- regular responsibility or schedule dependence;
- specialist or regulated work;
- physical, financial, or safeguarding risk;
- unpleasant or persistently unfilled maintenance;
- creation of commercial value;
- essential work on which the setting depends; and
- liability or outcome accountability.

The membrane is not a formula and has not been legally reviewed. Its purpose is to stop belonging-first language from laundering required work into “volunteering,” and to stop commerce from becoming the price of membership.

## 7. Maintenance, care, and hidden labour

The architecture rejects the assumption that maintenance will simply happen. Candidate responses are to simplify or redesign the activity, rotate a genuinely voluntary role, provide equipment, reduce frequency, resource a facilitator, or pay for required work.

Equal hours are not treated as the definition of equity. Capacity, access, risk, and life circumstances vary. At the same time, “different capacity” must not become a vague justification for relying indefinitely on the same people. Pilot A therefore records facilitator and maintenance burden at aggregate level and treats an informal hidden roster as a pause condition.

## 8. Access, safety, and differentiated participation

Belonging does not mean unrestricted access to every activity. Restrictions may be justified by consent, competence, age, safeguarding, equipment, risk, regulation, resource scarcity, or the rights of others. The reason, decision path, duration, and route to review should be explicit.

At least one Pilot A participation path must require no public introduction, no skills disclosure, no group task, no personal history, and no promise to return. Accessibility must be reviewed with people who have relevant lived experience; this document cannot substitute for that work.

## 9. Social agency and bidirectional belonging

The project distinguishes social exposure from chosen connection and belonging. Entering a room may carry an **interaction acquisition cost**: travel, unfamiliar entry, initiation, repeated attendance, sensory or communication load, money, time, transport, care responsibilities, anxiety, or uncertainty about reciprocity.

This leads to a developing question: is a person considered to belong only when they keep acquiring interaction, or can the setting also remember and invite them without guilt, chasing, or retention optimisation?

Pilot A includes only a minimal exploratory form of bidirectional belonging: an opt-in preference for one neutral reminder or invitation. It is not a retention target, and silence or refusal ends the contact. Whether proactive outreach lowers barriers or creates pressure is an open hypothesis.

## 10. Generative potential

A later project layer proposes a possible loop:

> curiosity → learning → capability → contribution → resources or products → new possibilities → curiosity

The loop may compound, stall, or fail. It is not claimed to be exponential, inevitable, or part of Pilot A. The governing sentence is:

> Curiosity can become productive without productivity becoming compulsory.

## 11. Architecture layers and maturity

| Layer | Current status |
|---|---|
| Human Foundations relationship | Separate conceptual relationship; no mutual validation |
| Curiosity Commons normative core | Current project commitments; human normative adoption required |
| Pilot A mechanism | Current manual-first protocol candidate; externally gated |
| Safeguards and instruments | Detailed prototypes; not certified or validated |
| Plural exchange and economic membrane | Project synthesis; outside Pilot A and legally unreviewed |
| Capacity-relative recognition and solidarity buffer | Unvalidated broader proposal; not a capacity-surveillance mandate |
| Bidirectional belonging and interaction acquisition cost | Developing hypothesis; minimally explored in Pilot A |
| Story Commons or comparative story work | Parked; culturally and consent sensitive; not released here |
| Participant-facing AI matching | Deliberately excluded from Pilot A; possible later module only after evidence and governance |
| Federation, enterprise, shared ownership, and replication | Unvalidated future work; no legal or financial structure adopted |

## 12. What this architecture does not establish

It does not establish that the combined model is novel, that people will feel they belong, that the board will be used, that capacity discovery produces beneficial connections, that refusal will remain penalty-free, that hidden labour can be contained, that the model scales, that an economic membrane works in law or practice, or that AI should later be added.

Those are questions for source review, external critique, careful piloting, and—in some cases—different projects entirely.
