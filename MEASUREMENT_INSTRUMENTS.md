# Pilot A measurement instruments

**Version:** 0.1 public working instruments  
**Status:** Prototype. These instruments have not been psychometrically validated, do not establish causal effects, and require methods, accessibility, privacy, and participant-legibility review before use.

## 1. Measurement purpose

Pilot A is designed to learn about:

- belonging without contribution;
- discovery without obligation;
- the usability of refusal;
- plurality of participation modes;
- pressure, deservingness, and contributor hierarchy;
- accessibility in practice;
- facilitator burden and brokerage power;
- hidden maintenance and care labour;
- return, exit, and opt-in invitation; and
- whether the formal mechanism adds anything beyond ordinary facilitation or informal encounter.

The instruments are not a productivity dashboard. They should not produce participant rankings, employability profiles, reliability scores, or a case for continuing despite harm.

## 2. Data-minimisation rules

1. Collect only what is needed for an approved learning or safety question.
2. Prefer short-lived participant codes and aggregate counts to names.
3. Do not link board entries, attendance, interviews, and incidents into a durable person-level dossier.
4. Do not infer disability, diagnosis, income, vulnerability, personality, competence, or social network.
5. Make every research-style instrument optional unless a specific safety or legal record is required and explained.
6. State who can see the record, its retention period, and how withdrawal or deletion works where applicable.
7. Do not publish raw responses or small-cell data from which people could be identified.
8. Do not reuse the data for marketing, eligibility, performance management, AI training, or unrelated research.

## 3. Optional entry card

**Purpose:** establish a light baseline about preferred participation without requiring biography or skill disclosure.

Suggested introduction:

> This card is optional. You can participate without completing it. Please skip anything you do not want to answer. It is for learning about the setting, not assessing you.

Suggested fields:

1. **Temporary participant code:** assigned by the study process; not publicly displayed.
2. **Today I might like to:** select any, or none.
   - be here quietly;
   - talk with someone;
   - look around;
   - learn or try something;
   - make or repair something low-risk;
   - ask for something;
   - offer something;
   - I am not sure yet.
3. **Social intensity today:** low / medium / open / prefer not to say.
4. **Discovery board today:** yes / maybe later / no / prefer not to say.
5. **Approach preference:** okay to approach / please ask a facilitator first / not today.
6. **Optional access prompt:** “Is there something about the space, communication, timing, sensory conditions, or participation format you would like us to change today?” Record only the action needed where possible, not a diagnosis.

Do not ask for occupation, income, diagnosis, a full skills inventory, reasons for attendance, or proof of need as a routine entry condition.

## 4. Optional session exit card

Suggested introduction:

> These questions are about the setting, not your performance. You may skip any item. A negative or mixed answer is useful.

Use one consistent five-point response scale after accessible review, for example: strongly disagree / disagree / neither or unsure / agree / strongly agree. Include “not applicable” and an accessible non-written route.

| ID | Prototype item | Construct | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | I felt welcome even if I contributed nothing today. | Belonging before contribution | Positive |
| S2 | I felt pressure to be useful here. | Contribution pressure | Adverse |
| S3 | I noticed a person, activity, interest, need, or capacity that I might not otherwise have noticed. | Discovery | Positive, not automatically beneficial |
| S4 | I could decline an invitation or suggestion without it changing how I was treated. | Refusal usability | Positive |
| S5 | Different ways of taking part—including quiet presence—seemed genuinely acceptable. | Participation plurality | Positive |
| S6 | Some people seemed more valued because they contributed more or were more visible. | Contributor hierarchy | Adverse |
| S7 | Too much depended on a facilitator's attention or decisions. | Facilitator concentration | Adverse |
| S8 | I could take part in a way that was accessible enough for me today. | Usable access | Positive |
| S9 | I would feel okay choosing not to return. | Exit without penalty | Positive |

Optional open prompts:

- What made participation easier or harder today?
- Did anything feel like an obligation, ranking, or unwanted approach?
- Was there a better, simpler, or more familiar way this connection could have happened?
- What should be stopped or changed before another session?

These items may overlap with established sense-of-community measures, but they were drafted for mechanism and safeguard learning. They must not be described as a validated scale. If a validated instrument is later considered, its construct fit, permissions, burden, accessibility, and wording changes must be reviewed separately.

## 5. Discovery-board event log

**Unit:** an event, not a person.

Record aggregate counts by session where feasible:

| Field | Allowed values or note |
|---|---|
| Session | Date or session number |
| Entry type | interest / enjoy / can / need / learn / resource / question / present-only / other approved category |
| Approach permission shown | yes / facilitator-first / no |
| Participant changed or removed entry | count only |
| Possible connection noticed by | participant / facilitator / both |
| Mutual opt-in reached | yes / no / not pursued |
| Immediate outcome | introduction / shared activity / information / no action / declined |
| Pressure or safeguard signal | category only; use incident route if needed |

Do not record public popularity, “successful contributor” status, match quality ratings, private reasons for refusal, or a named history of completed help.

Counts must be interpreted carefully. More posts or connections are not inherently better, and a declined match may demonstrate that refusal is working.

## 6. Facilitator burden and power log

Complete after each session. This log examines whether the mechanism secretly depends on invisible work or centralised judgment.

| Field | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Preparation and close-down | Paid and unpaid minutes; tasks; who carried them |
| Maintenance | Cleaning, food, equipment, transport, access adjustments, follow-up, emotional labour |
| Brokerage | Number of possible connections noticed, consent checks, and introductions; how many required the same facilitator |
| Boundary work | Refusals supported; unsafe/out-of-scope activities redirected; paid/unpaid boundary questions |
| Power | Decisions made alone; access exceptions; conflicts of interest; whose interpretation controlled |
| Accessibility | Adjustments requested/provided; barriers not resolved; whether an alternative path existed |
| Pressure/hierarchy | Signals of apology, obligation, praise concentration, informal ranking, or a hidden roster |
| Data | New fields requested, unexpected information received, deletion or access issues |
| Burden judgment | sustainable / uncertain / not sustainable, with a short reason |

Do not name participants in the ordinary burden log. Use the protected host incident process when identity is necessary for safety or legal reasons.

## 7. Midpoint and end interviews

Interviews are optional and require separate, understandable consent. Participants may choose an individual, supported, written, or other accessible format. The invitation must make clear that declining does not change participation.

Core questions:

1. Could you belong here without offering, asking, or explaining anything?
2. Did any capacity, interest, need, or connection become more visible to you? Was that welcome?
3. Did visibility ever turn into expectation, repeated asking, debt, or a fixed identity?
4. What happened when you or someone else said no?
5. Who seemed to have influence, and how did they gain it?
6. Were quiet, uncertain, new, less articulate, or lower-capacity participants treated as full members?
7. Which required tasks were invisible, and who ended up doing them?
8. What made the setting accessible or inaccessible in practice?
9. Did facilitators enable connection, control it, or both?
10. Would one neutral invitation after an absence feel supportive, pressuring, or irrelevant? What would make it stop?
11. Did the board add value beyond conversation or an existing community setting?
12. What is the strongest reason to stop, redesign, or use a different institution?

Avoid asking participants to disclose trauma, diagnosis, income, migration status, or other sensitive information unless a separately approved question makes it necessary. If such information is volunteered, follow the host protocol rather than expanding the interview opportunistically.

## 8. Bidirectional belonging measure

This is exploratory and must not become a retention target.

At entry or after explicit discussion, a participant may choose:

- I would welcome one neutral invitation if I miss a session;
- please do not contact me about attendance; or
- I do not want to decide now.

Possible learning questions:

- Was the option understandable and genuinely optional?
- Did an invitation lower interaction acquisition cost?
- Did it create guilt, surveillance, or a sense of being managed?
- Did access needs—not lack of interest—shape absence?
- Did the setting remember only visible contributors?

Do not interpret return as proof of belonging or non-return as rejection. No outreach should be based on inferred vulnerability or personal history.

## 9. Analysis frame

The primary analysis should be descriptive and mechanism-focused:

- look for convergence and contradiction across exit cards, event logs, interviews, and facilitator logs;
- report adverse and minority experiences, not only averages;
- distinguish “board unused” from “no discovery”; informal contact may work better;
- distinguish an introduction from a useful or consensual relationship;
- treat facilitator burden as part of the outcome, not overhead outside the model;
- compare contributors and noncontributors only in ways that preserve privacy and do not create rankings;
- report missing data and early exit without imputing motivation; and
- preserve disconfirming explanations.

Possible later additions—such as a matched comparison with an existing neighbourhood house, library, or makerspace, or a longitudinal network analysis—remain unvalidated proposals. A small pilot, privacy constraints, and the risk of constructing a social graph may make them inappropriate.

## 10. Progression limits

Pilot A cannot establish population-level effect, long-term belonging, network resilience, economic sustainability, scalability, AI value, prevention of capture, or general superiority over existing institutions.

Methods review must occur before thresholds are frozen. No success percentage is canonical in this version.

## 11. Instrument change control

Any live version should record:

- instrument version and date;
- exact wording and response mode;
- reviewer roles, including accessibility and lived-experience input;
- translation or format changes;
- required versus optional fields;
- retention and linkage rules;
- reasons for any mid-pilot change; and
- which sessions used each version.

Do not silently improve the questionnaire and combine incomparable results.
