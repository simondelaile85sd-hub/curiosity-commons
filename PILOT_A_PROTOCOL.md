# Pilot A protocol

**Version:** 0.2 public working protocol  
**Status:** Current protocol candidate for external design review. Not cleared for recruitment or operation. Not a service specification, legal opinion, ethics approval, safety certification, or validated intervention.

This protocol must be read with [SAFEGUARDS_PAUSE_AND_FAILURE_THRESHOLDS.md](SAFEGUARDS_PAUSE_AND_FAILURE_THRESHOLDS.md) and [MEASUREMENT_INSTRUMENTS.md](MEASUREMENT_INSTRUMENTS.md).

## 1. Pilot question

Can a small, low-pressure setting help adults discover useful connections, interests, needs, or capacities without making contribution the price of belonging?

## 2. Scope of the test

Pilot A tests only:

1. belonging-first participation;
2. optional, participant-controlled capacity and interest visibility; and
3. human-facilitated, mutual-opt-in connection.

It is not a test of a complete Curiosity Commons, an economic system, an AI matching system, a federation, a social-security design, or a substitute for professional services.

## 3. Working hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | What could count against it |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | People can experience welcome without contributing or disclosing a capacity | Noncontributors feel peripheral, watched, or less entitled to return |
| H2 | Optional visibility can support discovery without creating obligation | The board creates pressure, assignment, unwanted approaches, or a durable identity |
| H3 | Refusal can occur without penalty | “No” changes welcome, access, reputation, future invitations, or facilitator behaviour |
| H4 | Multiple legitimate participation modes can coexist at low pressure | Quiet, uncertain, less articulate, or lower-capacity participants are displaced by confident contributors |
| H5 | Manual facilitation can reveal the information and safeguards needed before any algorithmic layer is considered | Human brokerage becomes opaque, overcentralised, or too burdensome to learn from safely |

These hypotheses are not predictions of success. Evidence that an ordinary community institution or informal encounter works better is a useful result.

## 4. Intended setting

- one host venue;
- approximately 10–30 adults;
- 8–12 weeks;
- one half-day session per week, subject to host and participant review;
- a small, named facilitation team with cover and supervision; and
- a design that does not silently depend on participant labour to open, clean, coordinate, safeguard, or close the venue.

Adults are the default population. Inclusion of children or young people requires a different safeguarding, consent, staffing, and site protocol and is outside this version.

## 5. Entry conditions

Before recruitment, all hard gates in the safeguard document must be cleared for the specific host, jurisdiction, activities, facilitators, and participants. Required participant-facing materials must be reviewed for plain language, accessibility, consent, complaints, privacy, and withdrawal.

The host and project lead must be able to answer:

- Who has authority to run and stop the pilot?
- Who holds legal, insurance, work health and safety, privacy, and safeguarding responsibilities?
- Which activities are in scope, and who is competent to supervise them?
- What happens if a facilitator is absent?
- How can a participant complain, appeal, refuse, withdraw, or request an access adjustment?
- Which records exist, why, for how long, and who can see them?
- Which required tasks are paid, rostered, or held by the host rather than assumed to be volunteered?

## 6. Session components

### 6.1 Belonging-first arrival

Participants can enter without a public introduction, skills disclosure, group task, personal history, or promise to return. Facilitators explain that being present is enough and repeat the point through practice, not only signage.

At least one low-social-intensity or quiet participation path must be available. A communal drink or food offer may be unconditional if it can be provided safely and sustainably; it must not be exchanged for work.

### 6.2 Optional discovery board

The board may include prompts for interests, things someone enjoys, things they can do, things they want to learn, practical needs, resources, questions, or a preference to be present without posting.

Controls:

- posting is optional;
- the default time horizon is “today” or another short, explicit period;
- a participant may use a first name, pseudonym, or no public name, subject to the contact method chosen;
- each post states whether an approach is welcome and by what route;
- entries can be changed or removed at any time;
- entries expire and are not copied into a permanent capacity profile;
- no rating, reliability score, popularity count, productivity measure, or public completion record is attached;
- facilitators do not infer capacities, vulnerabilities, diagnoses, or availability; and
- posting does not create a promise, duty, debt, or entitlement.

### 6.3 Human-facilitated introductions

A facilitator may notice a possible connection, but first asks each person separately whether an introduction is wanted. Either person may decline without explanation. A declined match is not re-pitched unless the person initiates it later.

The facilitator's role is to support consent and legibility, not to allocate labour, judge worth, optimise throughput, or become the sole broker of relationships.

### 6.4 Low-risk activities

Activities should be small, reversible, and within the reviewed competence and risk envelope of the site. Examples might include conversation, explanation, demonstration, low-risk making or repair, shared inquiry, or learning alongside another person. The actual activity list must be approved locally; this protocol does not authorise any particular tool, food, transport, care, health, legal, financial, construction, electrical, child-related, or regulated activity.

### 6.5 Optional bidirectional invitation

A participant may opt in to one neutral reminder or invitation after an absence. This is exploratory only.

- No contact is sent without opt-in.
- One message is the default, not a campaign.
- No reason for absence is requested.
- No guilt, urgency, personalisation from inferred vulnerability, or retention optimisation is used.
- A decline, no-response preference, or opt-out ends the contact.
- Return rate is not treated as a belonging score.

## 7. Contribution and labour boundary

No participant is required to clean, host, facilitate, teach, post, cook, repair, document, or recruit in order to belong.

An offered contribution triggers review when it becomes substantial, repeated, scheduled, safety-critical, specialist, regulated, commercially valuable, physically risky, essential to operation, or accountable for outcomes. The response may be to stop, simplify, redesign, supervise, formally volunteer, or pay. Classification must be checked for the actual arrangement and jurisdiction.

Pilot A has no participant points, credits, debt ledger, labour quota, contribution target, or reward tier.

## 8. Access and activity boundaries

Unconditional belonging is compatible with activity-specific limits. A person may be unable to use a tool, enter a restricted area, handle another person's information, or lead a regulated activity while remaining equally welcome in the Commons.

Any restriction should have:

- a stated safety, consent, competence, legal, or scarcity reason;
- the least restrictive workable form;
- an accessible explanation;
- a review or appeal path where appropriate; and
- no conversion into a general status judgment.

## 9. Facilitator responsibilities

Facilitators are responsible for:

- explaining the belonging-first and refusal rules;
- maintaining multiple legitimate participation paths;
- checking mutual consent before introductions;
- noticing pressure, hierarchy, exclusion, hidden labour, and access barriers;
- applying the stop and pause rules;
- keeping only the approved minimal records;
- recording their own burden and brokerage dependence;
- referring matters outside scope rather than improvising professional services;
- declaring conflicts and not rewarding allies or articulate contributors; and
- making complaints, appeals, and withdrawal routes usable.

Facilitators must not diagnose, rank, assign, promise outcomes, create private dossiers, or use sensitive disclosures to drive matches.

## 10. Data boundary

Pilot records are limited to what is reasonably necessary for safety, operation, and the approved learning questions. The default is aggregate or short-lived information.

Prohibited by this protocol:

- personality or employability profiles;
- participant reputation or productivity histories;
- inferred vulnerability, capacity, or social graph;
- scraped or externally enriched data;
- recording private conversations by default;
- AI training use;
- publication of identifiable stories without a separate, specific rights process; and
- reuse of operational records for unrelated research, marketing, eligibility, or surveillance.

The final host-specific protocol must state retention periods, access roles, deletion processes, breach response, and whether the host is an entity covered by applicable privacy law.

## 11. Evaluation sequence

1. Complete an optional, minimal entry card.
2. Record aggregate board and connection events without reputation scoring.
3. Keep a facilitator burden and power log.
4. Use a short exit card after sessions.
5. Conduct optional midpoint and end interviews under separate consent.
6. Review stop and pause signals continuously, not only at the end.
7. Interpret non-use, refusal, absence, exit, and negative feedback as data rather than noncompliance.

No arbitrary success percentage is set before methods and host review. The pilot is not powered to establish population effects, and its prototype questions are not validated scales.

## 12. Deliberate exclusions and supersession

Compared with the 9 August 2026 minimum viable pilot canvas, this protocol deliberately removes:

- a participant-facing AI station;
- automated or algorithmic matching;
- points, credits, or internal currency;
- commerce or paid participant exchange inside the test mechanism;
- federation or cross-site operation;
- social-security or benefits mechanisms; and
- substitution for professional, public, or regulated services.

Those elements are not “missing.” They are broader or later project questions deliberately excluded to preserve causal legibility and reduce harm.

## 13. Decision rule after Pilot A

Progression requires both a meaningful signal and understood failure evidence. A positive average response is not enough if the mechanism also produces contribution pressure, unsafe practice, inaccessible participation, hidden rosters, a contributor hierarchy, facilitator domination, data creep, or dependence on unpaid essential work.

Valid outcomes include:

- continue with a frozen and reviewed protocol;
- repeat a redesigned manual pilot;
- retain only a subset of the mechanism;
- use or partner with an existing institution instead;
- stop because burden, risk, or coercion is not acceptable; or
- conclude that the question cannot be answered with this design.
