# Evidence, prior art, and provenance crosswalk

**Status:** Targeted public review updated 15 September 2026. This is not a systematic review, legal opinion, exhaustive novelty search, or proof that Curiosity Commons works.

## 1. Reading rule

Evidence must be transferred only to the mechanism or proposition actually studied. A source can establish that a practice, concept, instrument, or risk already exists without validating Curiosity Commons or Pilot A.

The absence of a source from this list does not establish novelty. The list is a starting crosswalk for external correction.

## 2. Prior-art crosswalk

|---|---|---|---|
| Garrett Hardin, “[The Tragedy of the Commons](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.162.3859.1243)” (1968) | A highly influential warning model about unrestrained use of a shared resource | A historical prompt to make resource, boundary, and governance assumptions explicit | That all commons fail; that Curiosity Commons is a classic common-pool resource; or that Hardin is the last word on commons governance |
| Elinor Ostrom, [*Governing the Commons*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763) (1990) | Empirical and institutional analysis of communities governing common-pool resources under varied conditions | Precedent for examining boundaries, rules, monitoring, sanctions, conflict resolution, and nested governance | Direct validation of a belonging-first social setting, Pilot A, or any exact Curiosity Commons mechanism |

## 3. Project synthesis versus prior art

Curiosity Commons does not claim invention of belonging, community space, volunteering, mutual aid, cooperative governance, mutual credit, adult learning, peer learning, capacity matching, community meals, repair, facilitation, or commons stewardship.

The present project synthesis combines:

- unconditional belonging;
- optional and revisable capacity visibility;
- a noncompulsory pathway from encounter and learning toward possible contribution and earning;
- plural exchange modes rather than one universal ledger;
- refusal without penalty;
- equal standing despite unequal achievement;
- an economic membrane between belonging and formal work or commerce;
- explicit monitoring of hidden labour, contributor hierarchy, facilitator power, accessibility, and capture;
- interaction-acquisition-cost and bidirectional-belonging questions; and
- a manual-first pilot that must be able to fail safely and informatively.

That combination may be useful, redundant, already instantiated elsewhere, internally contradictory, or impractical. A systematic review and field comparison have not yet established its novelty or added value.

| AI-assisted operationalisation | document structures; narrowed Pilot A design; deliberate removal of participant-facing AI from Pilot A; board expiry and approach controls; stop/pause taxonomy; prototype measurement wording; traceability and hostile-adoption tables; literature discovery; reviewer packaging; consistency, privacy, supersession, and publication checks |

| External validation | none yet for Curiosity Commons or Pilot A |

AI assistance does not constitute authorship of Simon Delaile's normative choices, evidence, rights clearance, external review, or validation. Simon's approval of an operationalisation makes it project material; it does not turn a proposal into a finding.

## 5. Evidence-transfer limits

1. A successful manual introduction would support only that mechanism in that context—not a whole Commons, an AI layer, or scale.
2. A validated external scale does not validate locally drafted questions.
3. A cooperative principle can inform governance without making the project a cooperative.
4. Commons research can motivate governance questions without proving the project manages a classic common-pool resource.
5. Time-bank evidence is adjacent to, not evidence for, Pilot A's no-credit design.
6. Volunteer standards can guide practice without resolving employment status or WHS duties.
7. Human Foundations propositions do not validate Curiosity Commons; Pilot A findings do not validate Human Foundations as a whole.
8. A positive pilot finding does not establish long-term, causal, comparative, population, economic, or scalable effects.

## 6. Competing explanations to preserve

- Ordinary good facilitation, not the architecture, produces any reported benefit.
- Informal encounter works better than the board.
- The board creates observation and pressure even when optional.
- Existing neighbourhood houses, libraries, community centres, or makerspaces already provide the useful mechanism.
- More articulate or confident participants gain visibility and influence.
- Reciprocity norms create debt without a formal ledger.
- Facilitator labour makes the small pilot appear more scalable than it is.
- People who face the highest interaction acquisition costs are least visible in the data.
- Accessibility changes may reveal that there is no single low-pressure format.
- Recognition may convert contribution into governance power over time.

## 7. Source-review gaps

The next review should deepen, at minimum:

- Australian and international neighbourhood-house and community-centre practice;
- mutual aid, repair cafés, libraries of things, makerspaces, men's sheds, community kitchens, and peer-learning models;
- disability justice, universal and inclusive design, transport access, and low-social-intensity participation;
- proactive outreach, social prescribing, community navigation, and bidirectional belonging;
- volunteer, employment, tax, charity, insurance, safeguarding, and WHS law in the host jurisdiction;
- informal power, founder effects, democratic participation, platform/cooperative governance, and mission locks;
- social-network measurement ethics and the privacy cost of structural analysis;
- Indigenous data sovereignty, cultural authority, and community-led story governance; and
- rights and permissions for any external measurement instrument.

Corrections are welcome through [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). A source should be connected to the precise proposition it supports, and a missing source should not be treated as proof of novelty.
